LOFO EVALUATION — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Protocol: Leave-One-Family-Out, 7 folds (F1–F7), Isolation Forest retrained
fresh per fold on CICIoT2023 with the held-out family fully excluded from
training. BenignTraffic retained in every fold. XGBoost (fixed, pretrained,
34-class) not retrained. All 7 folds completed successfully — no fold
failures, no NaN offset_, no fallback substitutions were used anywhere.

AGGREGATE NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                          Isolation Forest alone     Hybrid (XGB conf. OR IF)
Macro UTDR (mean ± std)   0.4812 ± 0.4111            0.5112 ± 0.4369
Macro Precision           0.0592 ± 0.0522            0.0616 ± 0.0532
Macro F1                  0.0745 ± 0.0984            0.0759 ± 0.0991
Macro FPR                 0.0500 ± 0.0001            0.0547 ± 0.0074

Micro UTDR (pooled)       0.0204                     0.0218
Micro Precision           0.0619                     0.0631
Micro F1                  0.0306                     0.0325
Micro FPR                 0.0500                     0.0526

Micro and macro diverge sharply (macro UTDR ~48–51% vs. micro UTDR ~2%)
because 96% of all test-unknown samples across the 7 folds belong to the
three families the detector fails on hardest (DDoS, DoS, Mirai). Macro
aggregation treats all 7 families as equally important; micro aggregation
reflects what would actually happen if a random flow's family were unknown,
weighted by how common each family's traffic actually is.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PER-FOLD OUTLIERS (|z| > 2 relative to the 7-fold macro mean/std)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Isolation Forest alone:
  F6 (Spoofing) — precision z=2.03, F1 z=2.09 (highest of all folds on both)
Hybrid (XGB conf. OR IF):
  F6 (Spoofing) — F1 z=2.08 (precision z=1.96, just under threshold)
  F1 (DDoS) — FPR z=2.44 (hybrid's confidence-based flagging raises FPR
    specifically on this fold's very large known-set)

No fold is a UTDR outlier by the strict |z|>2 test, because the 7-fold UTDR
distribution is bimodal rather than clustered around its mean — three folds
sit far below the mean (F1, F2, F3) and four sit far above it (F4, F5, F6,
F7), which inflates the standard deviation enough that no single value
crosses 2 std. This is a case where the underlying bimodal split is the
more important and more honest finding than the z-score screen: DDoS, DoS,
and Mirai form one cluster (UTDR 0.1–3.4%); Recon, Web, Spoofing, and
BruteForce form another (UTDR 67–95%).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STRONGEST AND WEAKEST FOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Strongest: F6 — Spoofing (DNS_Spoofing, MITM-ArpSpoofing).
  UTDR 92.9%/95.0%, Precision 16.5%/16.6%, F1 0.280/0.282 — best on every
  metric except being tied near the ~5% FPR floor common to all folds.
  Per-class: MITM-ArpSpoofing individually reaches 95.7% (IF alone) / 97.4%
  (hybrid) flag rate; DNS_Spoofing reaches 88.0% / 90.9%.

Weakest: F1 — DDoS (12 flood-attack classes, 855,981 test-unknown samples,
  by far the largest fold).
  UTDR 0.14%/0.26%, F1 0.0028/0.0050 — the lowest F1 of all 7 folds despite
  not having the single lowest precision (F7 is lower on precision alone).
  F2 (DoS, UTDR 0.80%/0.85%) fails for the identical structural reason and is
  nearly as weak — both are flood-pattern families that remain heavily
  represented in "known" training via other flood-type attacks not held out
  in that fold, so the held-out flood variant does not look anomalous to the
  fold's Isolation Forest.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOES THIS SUPPORT A "ZERO-DAY" CLAIM?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No — not as a general claim, and this LOFO run is the direct evidence for
why. The manuscript's original 679-sample single-split result (95.14% hybrid
UTDR) used exactly seven rare, low-volume, behaviorally distinct classes
(Backdoor_Malware, BrowserHijacking, CommandInjection, SqlInjection, XSS,
Uploading_Attack, Recon-PingSweep) as "unknown" — every one of which falls
into this LOFO run's high-performing cluster (F4/F5, UTDR 67–95%). No
DDoS, DoS, or Mirai variant was ever tested as unknown in the original
evaluation, and those are precisely the families this LOFO run shows the
detector cannot recognize as novel (UTDR 0.1–3.4%) — because they resemble,
in flood-pattern feature space, other attack families the model has already
seen. The honest characterization is: this Isolation Forest / hybrid
approach can flag traffic from a genuinely novel BEHAVIORAL CATEGORY
(application-layer exploitation, spoofing, reconnaissance, credential
attacks) with moderate-to-good recall but poor precision; it CANNOT reliably
flag a novel variant of a volumetric flood attack as unknown when other
flood attacks are already in its training distribution. "Zero-day
detection" as an unqualified claim is not supportable. A qualified claim
("unknown-traffic screening, effective for behaviorally novel attack
categories, unreliable for novel flood-type variants") is supportable and
is what these results actually show.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDATION FOR THE MANUSCRIPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Keyword: replace "zero-day unknown traffic screening" with "unknown-traffic
screening" throughout (title, abstract, keywords). Do not use "zero-day" as
an unqualified capability claim anywhere the LOFO results are cited.

Contribution 4 (§1.5): rewrite to state that unknown-traffic detection was
evaluated via a 7-fold leave-one-family-out protocol (not a single 679-
sample holdout), and that detection performance is strongly family-
dependent: high recall (67–95% UTDR) for behaviorally distinct novel
categories (reconnaissance, web exploitation, spoofing, credential attacks),
near-zero recall (0.1–3.4% UTDR) for novel volumetric flood-attack variants,
and low precision throughout (0.5–16.6%) due to class-imbalance base rates.
Report both macro (0.48–0.51 mean UTDR) and micro (0.02 pooled UTDR)
aggregates explicitly, since they answer different questions and diverge by
more than 20x.

Do not keep the original 679-sample result as an equally-weighted
alternative next to LOFO — it is not a valid baseline for the same claim,
since it only ever tested the "easy" cluster of classes. If retained at all,
it should be clearly labeled as a "best-case, non-representative single-
split result on 7 pre-selected easy classes" and the LOFO protocol should be
presented as the primary, methodologically sound evaluation replacing it.
