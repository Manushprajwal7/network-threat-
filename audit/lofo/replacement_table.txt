================================================================================
INTERPRETATION
================================================================================
The macro-average UTDR (48–51%) conceals a bimodal split that the single
679-sample holdout in the original manuscript could not reveal: three
high-volume, flood-style families (DDoS, DoS, Mirai — 96% of all test-unknown
samples across the seven folds) are detected at near-zero rates (0.1–3.4%
UTDR) when held out, because the Isolation Forest's decision boundary is
shaped by the many structurally similar flood-type attacks that remain in
training; four low-volume, behaviorally distinct families (Recon, Web,
Spoofing, BruteForce) are detected at 67–95% UTDR, because their traffic
patterns differ sharply from the flood-dominated training distribution.
Precision is low in every fold (0.5–16.6%) regardless of family, reflecting
the base-rate problem inherent to anomaly detection on a dataset this
imbalanced: even a fixed ~5% false-positive rate on hundreds of thousands to
over a million known samples yields tens of thousands of false alarms, which
swamps true positives except in the largest unknown-family folds. The hybrid
method (XGBoost confidence OR Isolation Forest) improves UTDR modestly over
Isolation Forest alone in every fold (macro UTDR 51.1% vs. 48.1%) at a small
cost in FPR, confirming the fusion rule adds value but does not change the
qualitative pattern. These results do not support treating the manuscript's
original 95.14% hybrid UTDR as representative: that number reflects the
specific seven rare classes chosen for the single-split holdout, all of which
happen to fall in the "easy" (behaviorally distinct) category — no
DDoS/DoS/Mirai class was ever tested as unknown in the original evaluation.
